CHAPTER 11: WORLD OSSUARY

SCENE 1: THE INVITATION

She hasn't moved in days. The ceiling has become a map of all the places she will never go. James enters with coffee, the smell too bright, too alive. He sits beside the couch.

"I thought we could go into town today. Market's open. Get some air."

The words land on her like ash. She doesn't respond.

His hand on hers: warm, patient, impossible. "Persephone. When's the last time you ate something I didn't bring you?"

"I'm not hungry."

"That's not what I asked."

The silence stretches. Through the window, November light the color of old bones. She blinks. Refocuses. Sees his face waiting, worried, weighted with something she can no longer name.

"Okay," she says. Why? Exhaustion. Inability to refuse. Some faint animal hope that normalcy might return if she pretends hard enough.

He smiles—relief flooding his features. She looks away.

SCENE 2: ARRIVAL AT MARKET

The truck ride: trees passing like corpses in a procession. Skeletal branches. Dead leaves clinging. Fungal rot blooming at the roots. She catalogues their deaths—oak blight, ash borer, climate stress. The implant whispers data she doesn't want.

*Quercus rubra. Projected lifespan reduced 40% due to regional temperature increase. Mortality rate accelerating.*

James parks. Through the windshield: bunting. Folk music. Families. A small-town tableau, cheerful and bright. She sees a slaughterhouse.

"You okay?" he asks.

She nods. Lies.

He helps her out. Her legs are unsteady—hasn't walked far in days. He holds her elbow, guides her toward the entrance. The noise hits first: too many voices, too much laughter, the mandolin's bright strum like needles in her skull. Then the colors: orange pumpkins, red apples, yellow squash. Everything screaming Look at me I'm alive I'm alive I'm—

The implant stutters. Text overlays her vision:

*TARGET ACQUIRED: 47 Homo sapiens. STATUS: Viable. PROJECTED LIFESPAN: 12-83 years. MORTALITY RATE: 100%.*

She blinks. Can't dismiss it. The notification hangs there, translucent, damning.

SCENE 3: FIRST BREAKDOWN — VEGETABLE STALLS

They enter the first row. Produce stalls. A vendor holds up an heirloom tomato, grinning: "First of the season!"

Persephone sees it dying.

*Solanum lycopersicum. Time since harvest: 14 hours. Cellular degradation: 8%. Estimated time to decomposition: 72 hours.*

She reaches out. Touches it. The world inverts.

Roots cut. Stem bleeding. Sun-starved cells gasping. It's still alive. Dying but alive. Everything here is dying and they're selling it and smiling and—

"Beautiful, right? We grew these ourselves—organic, no pesticides."

Her hand trembles on the tomato's skin. She feels its death beneath her fingertips, warm and wet and wrong.

"Persephone?" James's voice from far away. "You okay?"

She pulls back. Stumbles. They move past the second stall—squash, carrots, lettuce piled in baskets—and every vegetable is screaming. Not sound. Worse. Cellular memory: the carrot torn from soil, mycorrhizal family left behind to die. The lettuce still photosynthesizing, chloroplasts not yet aware they're dead.

Green. So much green. Chlorophyll. Life. No—death pretending to be life. Photosynthesis stopped. Mitochondria failing. We stack corpses in baskets and call them fresh.

Her knees weaken. James catches her arm. "Hey, hey—let's slow down."

She can't slow down. The world is a cascade of endings she can't unsee.

SCENE 4: SECOND BREAKDOWN — MEAT & FISH

James tries to steer her away—toward the craft stalls, pottery, textiles, anything without a nervous system. But they pass the butcher's tent. She sees the carcasses.

A pig. Whole. Pink. Headless. Split down the center, hanging from a hook like a verdict.

Her vision locks. Can't look away.

*TARGET IDENTIFIED: Sus scrofa domesticus. STATUS: Deceased. CAUSE OF DEATH: Mechanical exsanguination. ESTIMATED AGE AT DEATH: 6 months. NATURAL LIFESPAN: 15-20 years. EFFICIENCY RATIO: 4.1% lifespan utilized.*

She hears it. Not with her ears—with something deeper. The implant translates cellular memory: squealing recorded in muscle tissue, terror encoded in every fiber. The heart's last beats, electrical echoes still resonating. Maternal pheromones—the sow was pregnant.

*10 unborn piglets. Terminated.*

Persephone gasps. Doubles over. James pulls her, urgent now, but she's already seen the fish stall.

Salmon. Trout. Mackerel on ice, mouths gaping, eyes clouded. The Devonian flashes through her: fish crawling onto land 375 million years ago. Tiktaalik. Lobe-finned ancestor.

*We came from you. And now we stack you on ice and charge $18/lb.*

She sees their suffocation—gills collapsing, blood oxygen plummeting, death by slow asphyxiation. The implant provides taxonomy like a eulogy:

*Kingdom Animalia. Phylum Chordata. Class Actinopterygii. Family: my family. Cousins.*

We're eating our cousins.

Chicken carcasses in plastic. Trussed. Sanitized.

*Gallus gallus domesticus. Descendant of Burmese jungle fowl. 50 billion slaughtered annually. Passenger Pigeon: extinct. Carolina Parakeet: extinct. This market is a mass extinction event in miniature.*

She collapses. Knees hit dirt. Hyperventilating. But not crying—laughing. Manic, broken laughter spilling out like blood.

"Persephone, look at me—"

She does. Sees James—bones beneath skin, future corpse, inevitable decay. The implant calculates his remaining years, probability of cancer, cardiac failure, the slow dimming of synapses. She laughs harder, can't stop.

Onlookers gathering. Murmurs. *Is she okay? Should we call someone?*

James kneels, lifts her bodily. She's limp, eyes open but not seeing. He carries her toward the parking lot. A vendor calls after, concerned. He doesn't answer.

SCENE 5: THIRD BREAKDOWN — THE CROWD

The truck. James sits her in the passenger seat, brings water from the cab. She doesn't drink. He kneels outside the open door, holding her hand.

"Breathe. Just breathe."

She's staring past him. Back at the market.

From here, elevated, she sees the people eating. A child with vanilla ice cream dripping down their chin. A man biting into a hot dog, teeth tearing the casing. A couple sharing fried dough, sugar dusting their lips.

They're all mouths. Consuming. Excreting. Consuming. Biological imperative without end.

*Ares exploded and they bought hot dogs. Iphy drowned and the world kept eating.*

Children playing tag, shrieking with joy. She sees future corpses. Climate wars. Starvation. Migration camps. These children won't live to old age—collapse is coming and they don't know, can't know, are innocent in their doomed laughter.

*Anthropocene. Named after us. The era of human dominance. But we're the extinction event. We eat the world and then we'll starve.*

Laughter again—then sobbing—then laughter, the sounds tangled, incoherent. She grabs James's face, makes him look.

"Don't you see it? Don't you see what we are?"

He looks. Sees stalls, families, cheerful normalcy. "I see people living."

"No." Her voice raw. "We're dying. All of us. We've always been dying."

James's eyes—helpless, aching. "Then let's go home."

The fight drains out of her. She slumps back. He closes the door, rounds the truck, starts the engine. They drive in silence.

SCENE 6: JAMES CARES FOR HER

The farmhouse. Afternoon light slanting through the windows. James helps her inside, lays her on the couch, covers her with a blanket. She stares at the ceiling—the same map of places she'll never go.

He makes tea. Chamomile, honey. Brings it to her. She doesn't drink.

He sits on the floor beside the couch, holds her hand. Doesn't speak. What could he say?

She whispers: "I saw them all dying."

"Who?"

"Everyone. The fish. The pig. The children. You."

"I'm not dying."

She finally looks at him. "You are. Just slower."

James, quiet: "Then we're all dying together. You're not alone."

The words should comfort. They don't. She thinks: *He brought me to them. The cult. Apron Man. The drugging. He's complicit. But he's also here. Holding my hand. Both things are true.*

Time passes. Sun moves across the floor. Shadows lengthen. James falls asleep sitting against the couch, head tilted back, exhausted.

Persephone watches his chest rise and fall.

*Respiration. Oxidation. Mitochondrial decay. He's burning himself to stay alive. We're all just slow fires.*

She closes her eyes.

SCENE 7: MIKE'S SHADOW

Dusk. Or the next day. Time is fluid in her dissociation.

James is gone—buying groceries? Asleep upstairs? She doesn't remember.

Orange light through the window. The same orange as the explosion. Ares burning across the sky.

Her phone buzzes on the coffee table. She's been ignoring it for days, weeks. Doesn't reach for it now.

But the implant does.

*INCOMING MESSAGE: SENDER IDENTIFIED: Mike H.*

*Message preview: "I'm coming to Maine. We need to talk."*

Her eyes widen. First genuine emotion since the market—not fear, not relief. Dread.

She sits up. First voluntary movement in days. Reaches for the phone, reads the full message:

*"Persephone, I know you're with James. I know what happened with Ares. I'm coming to get you. We can fix this. —Mike"*

Her hand trembles.

*He survived. He's alive. He shouldn't be alive.*

Mike. Old life. Miami. Marriage. Iphy's death. Blame and guilt knotted so tight she can't tell whose is whose.

Mike. Agamemnon. Returns from war expecting welcome, will find betrayal.

Mike. Intrusion. She's already too far gone. There's no fixing anything.

*He's coming. He'll ruin everything. Or he'll save everything. I don't know which is worse.*

She stands. First time in days she's chosen action. Walks to the window, looks out at the darkening forest.

Whispers: "Agamemnon returns."

Blackout.
