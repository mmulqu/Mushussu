Ch12 Agamemnon's Return

SCENE 1: MIKE'S ARRIVAL

She was on the couch again. Or still. Time had collapsed into a single endless afternoon, light moving across the walls like something alive, like something hunting. James was in the kitchen making soup—she could hear the knife against the cutting board, rhythmic, hypnotic, the sound of domesticity pretending everything was fine.

It wasn't fine. Nothing was fine. But James tried anyway, which made it worse somehow. Made it obscene.

The sound of tires on gravel reached her before her implant did. James froze mid-chop. They both heard it: engine dying, car door opening, footsteps on the porch stairs.

Her implant flickered: *FACIAL RECOGNITION PROCESSING... MATCH FOUND. CASTELLANOS, MICHAEL JOSEPH. PROBABILITY: 0.97. STATUS: NON-OPTIMAL. FLIGHT RISK ASSESSMENT: CRITICAL.*

The door opened without a knock.

Mike stood in the frame like something assembled from memory, not quite right. Too thin. Unshaven. Eyes hollow as bird bones. His clothes hung wrong, stained with something—coffee? dirt? blood?—she couldn't tell. Didn't want to know.

James moved first. "Can I help you?"

Mike's eyes slid past him like he wasn't there. Found her on the couch. "Perse. I need you to come with me."

Her mouth went dry. "Mike. How did you—"

"You told me." His voice flat. Affect all wrong. "Before. Remember? You said if everything fell apart, you'd go back to the coast. Count the birds."

She'd never said that. Had she? Memory was unreliable now, corroded at the edges, eaten through with holes.

James stepped between them. "I think you should leave."

Mike finally looked at him. Really looked. Like noticing furniture had moved. "Who the fuck are you?"

"I'm the person who lives here."

"This is Persephone's place now." Mike's voice still flat. Dead. "I'm taking her home."

"Mike." She forced words through her throat. "The rocket. Weren't you—?"

"They took me off." He said it like reporting weather. "Last minute. Replaced me. Then it exploded. They're all dead."

A pause. "I wasn't on it."

The soup pot boiled over. James didn't move. Steam rose behind him like smoke from an altar, sacrifice burning, offering refused.

"You need to leave," James said again. Firmer now. But his hand shook.

Mike walked past him. Sat on the floor next to the couch, back against the wall, knees drawn up. Like a child. Like someone who'd forgotten how to be a man.

"Perse," he said to the air. "Do you remember Iphy's laugh? I can't—I used to hear it all the time. In my head. Now it's gone. I can't remember her laugh."

Something broke inside her chest. Small. Clean. Final.


SCENE 2: HUSBAND'S ARRIVAL

They stayed like that. Frozen tableau. James in the kitchen, knife still in hand. Mike on the floor, staring at nothing. Persephone on the couch, watching herself not know what to do.

The second car arrived fifteen minutes later. Or fifteen hours. Time was unreliable.

The door opened again—Mike had left it ajar, like leaving an escape route.

A man entered. Clean-shaven. Pressed shirt wrinkled from travel. Dark circles under his eyes. He saw Mike on the floor and his whole body sagged with relief and terror mixed in equal measure.

"Oh thank god." He moved toward Mike, crouched down. "Mike. Mike, look at me. Baby, look at me."

Mike didn't respond. Just stared at the wall, at something beyond the wall, at the shape of everything ending.

The man turned to James and Persephone. His eyes were bloodshot. "I'm sorry. I'm his ex-husband. He left three days ago. He's not well."

"We can see that," James said.

"I've been trying to find him. He turned off his phone. I called hospitals, police stations—" His voice cracked. He pressed his palm against his eyes. Breathed. "I didn't know where else to look."

Persephone found her voice. "He just got here. We were... we were just talking."

The man looked at her then. Really looked. "You're Persephone."

It wasn't a question.

"From the lab," he continued. "You worked with him. Before."

She nodded.

"I'm David." He said it like confessing something. "He talks about you. A lot. After—" He stopped himself. Glanced at Mike. "After everything happened. With your daughter."

The air left the room.

"He needs help," David said. "Professional help. I've been trying to get him to go to a hospital but he won't—he thinks they're watching him. That NASA is tracking him. That the—" He stopped. Shook his head. "Please. Just let me take him home."

Mike spoke without moving. "I'm not leaving without Persephone."

"Mike—"

"She's the only one who understands. About Iphy."

David's face crumpled. He caught it, held it together through sheer will. "Persephone doesn't need to come. Just you and me. Like we planned. Remember? We were going to get you help. You promised."

"I didn't promise anything."

"You did. In the car. Before you—" David's jaw clenched. "Before you left."

Mike finally looked at him. Empty eyes. "That was before I knew where she was."


SCENE 3: JAMES INTERVENES

The soup had stopped boiling. James turned off the stove. Moved into the living room, still holding the knife. He'd forgotten he was holding it. Or maybe he hadn't.

"I think you both need to leave," he said. "Persephone's not going anywhere."

David stood. "I'm not trying to take her. I just want to take Mike home."

"She's staying here."

"I didn't—" David's face shifted. Confusion. "I didn't ask her to come. I'm asking him to come."

"You people need to leave. Now."

"Excuse me?"

"I don't know what you want from her," James said, "but she's been through enough. Mike showing up here—this isn't okay."

"James, stop—" Persephone tried to sit up. Failed. Her body had forgotten the mechanics of intervention.

"No." James's voice hardened. "You don't owe them anything. Not after what happened to—" He stopped. Too late.

David went very still. "After what?"

"After what happened to your daughter."

The temperature dropped ten degrees. Persephone felt it, physical thing, ice forming in her veins.

"How do you know about that?" David's voice barely above whisper.

"Persephone told me."

David turned to her. She watched him reassemble the situation in real time, saw the gears turning behind his eyes. "You told him?"

"I—yes."

"You never told anyone. Not even the therapist. Not even your mother."

She had no answer. The truth was complicated. The truth was drugged confessions and cult ideology and corruption she didn't understand. The truth would take hours to explain and she didn't have hours. Didn't have minutes. Had only this moment, narrowing to a point.

"What else did you tell him?" David asked. His voice had gone cold. Surgical.

"David—"

"What else?"


SCENE 4: THE ACCUSATION

David stepped toward James. "Who are you? Really?"

James lifted his chin. "I already told you."

"No. You told me you live here. You told me she's staying with you. You didn't tell me why she's here. In the middle of nowhere. Hiding."

"She's not hiding—"

"Then what is she doing?"

"That's between us."

"Is it?" David's hands curled into fists. "Because it seems like she came here running from something. From someone. And you just happened to be here."

James said nothing.

"What are you?" David pressed. "Some kind of—what, cult? One of those network groups?"

James went very still. Prey instinct.

"Oh my god." David's voice dropped. "You are. You're one of them."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"Yes you do. Mike told me. Before he left, before his breakdown got bad, he said Persephone was talking about some animal consciousness group. Drugging people. Making them think they could talk to birds. Talk to the fucking trees. That's you, isn't it?"

"It's not like that—"

"Did you drug her?"

Silence.

"Did you drug her?"

"It was consensual—"

David moved so fast James didn't have time to react. Grabbed him by the shirt, slammed him against the wall. The knife clattered to the floor.

"She can't consent when she's having a breakdown!" David shouted. "She can't consent when she's running from her life! When she's—"

"David, stop—" Persephone tried to stand. Her legs wouldn't work. She fell back onto the couch, weightless, powerless, watching everything happen through glass.

David's face was inches from James's. "You took her away. From her job. From her life. From the people who actually care about her."

"I saved her—"

"You destroyed her! Look at her! Does she look saved to you?"

James's eyes found hers. She saw it then: doubt. First time. He'd believed his own mythology until this moment, until someone named it for what it was.

"And Mike," David continued, voice breaking. "Mike was fine until she left the lab. Then she disappeared. Then he started losing it. NASA took him off the mission because he was obsessed with finding her. His entire career—gone. His life—gone. And now he's—"

He gestured at Mike still sitting on the floor, dissociated, gone.

"That's not my fault," James whispered.

"Isn't it?"


SCENE 5: THE MURDER

"You broke him," David said. Not shouting anymore. Worse than shouting. "Everything he worked for. Everything we built. Gone. Because you took her away. Because you filled her head with your—"

"I didn't break anyone—"

"HE LOST EVERYTHING BECAUSE OF YOU!"

David's hands found James's throat.

It happened too fast. Too slow. Both at once. Persephone watched it frame by frame and all at once, her implant stuttering, trying to process, failing: *VIOLENCE DETECTED. INTERVENTION RECOMMENDED. AUTONOMIC RESPONSE: FREEZE. OVERRIDE UNAVAILABLE.*

James's hands came up, clawing at David's wrists. His mouth opened. No sound. Just air that couldn't move, trapped, dying.

"Stop—stop—" Persephone heard herself screaming but the sound was far away, underwater, meaningless.

James's face went red. Purple. His eyes bulged. He twisted, trying to break free, knocked over the lamp. It shattered. Nobody noticed.

David's face was calm. That was the worst part. Not rage. Just calm. Determination. Someone doing something necessary, something final.

"David—DAVID STOP—"

James's legs kicked. Weaker now. His hands fell from David's wrists, grasped at air, at nothing. Found the knife on the floor. Fingers closed around the handle.

David saw it. Yanked James away from the knife, slammed him against the wall again. Harder. James's head cracked against drywall. The knife fell again.

"You destroyed everything," David said. Voice steady. Conversational. "Everything we had. Everything we were. Gone."

James's lips were blue.

Persephone tried to stand. Fell. Crawled toward them. Too slow. Her body wouldn't obey. The implant: *SYSTEM OVERRIDE RECOMMENDED. EMERGENCY SERVICES NOTIFICATION: AVAILABLE. SELECT ACTION—*

She couldn't select. Couldn't move. Could only watch.

James stopped struggling.

David held on thirty seconds longer. Making sure.

Then released.

James collapsed. Slid down the wall. Sat there, head tilted wrong, eyes open, looking at nothing.

The room was silent except for David's breathing. Ragged. Wet.

Mike watched from the floor. He hadn't moved. Hadn't intervened. Hadn't done anything at all.


SCENE 6: AFTERMATH

David stared at his hands. They were shaking. Red marks on his knuckles. Scratches from where James had tried to pry them off.

"Oh god," he whispered. "Oh god oh god oh god—"

Mike stood up. Finally moved. Finally present. "We should go now."

David looked at him. "What?"

"We should go. Before someone sees."

"I killed him." David's voice hollow. "I killed him. We have to—I have to call—"

"No." Mike's voice was firm. Decisive. The psychotic break had burned through to something underneath, something hard and clear. "No police."

"Mike, I murdered him—"

"He was going to kill you." Mike gestured at the knife on the floor. "You saw it. Persephone saw it. Self-defense."

"That's not—" David's face crumpled. "That's not what happened—"

"That's what happened." Mike moved to the door. "Perse, get your things. We're leaving."

Persephone stared at James's body. His eyes were still open. She should close them. That's what people did, wasn't it? In movies. In books. Close the dead person's eyes.

She didn't move.

"Persephone." Mike's voice. "We need to go."

"We have to call the police," she heard herself say. Autopilot. Social script.

"No."

"James is dead. We have to—"

"He drugged you. He manipulated you. He brought you into a cult. Do you want to explain that to the police? Do you want them asking why you're here? What you've been doing?"

She opened her mouth. Closed it.

Mike crouched in front of her. His eyes were clear now. Focused. "David was protecting you. That's all that happened. He saw James hurting you and he protected you. And now we need to leave before someone finds out."

"But—"

"No buts. Get up. Get your things. We're going."

David hadn't moved. Still staring at his hands. "I can't—I need to—"

Mike grabbed his arm. Gently. "Baby, look at me. Look at me. We're going to be okay. But we need to leave. Right now."

"I killed him."

"I know."

"I can't—"

"You can. For me. Please."

Something in David broke. Or mended. Hard to tell. He nodded.


SCENE 7: THE CHOICE

Persephone looked at James's body. At David shaking. At Mike, suddenly lucid, suddenly in command. Her implant cycled through options: *EMERGENCY SERVICES: 911. WITNESS PROTOCOL: STANDARD. FLIGHT RISK ASSESSMENT: HIGH. MORAL CALCULUS: LOADING... ERROR. DEONTOLOGY: CALL POLICE. UTILITARIANISM: MINIMIZE HARM. VIRTUE ETHICS: UNKNOWABLE. SYSTEM ERROR ERROR ERROR—*

She closed her eyes.

*If I call police: David arrested. Murder charge. Mike institutionalized. James still dead. Nothing fixed. Nothing saved.*

*If I don't: Complicit. Accessory. But David might survive this. Mike might survive this.*

*James drugged me. Groomed me. Used me for his ideology. Does he deserve justice? Do I owe him that?*

*David killed to protect Mike. That's love. Terrible love. Love that destroys. But love.*

*Mike is broken because the systems broke him. NASA. Capitalism. The machines that grind people into paste. The same machines that killed Iphy.*

*I can't save James.*

*Maybe I can save them.*

She opened her eyes. "Okay."

Mike stared at her. "Okay what?"

"Okay, we go. But not together." Her voice steadier now. Decision made. Point of no return passed. "You two take David's car. Drive south. Find a hospital in Massachusetts. Tell them David had a breakdown. Check him in."

"What about you?" Mike asked.

"I'll stay. Clean up. Make it look like James left. Then I'll go into the woods."

"We can't just leave you—" David finally spoke. His voice broken glass.

"You have to. If we're all together, they'll find us. If we separate, I can—" She swallowed. "I can handle it."

"The cult," Mike said. Flat statement. "They'll find you."

"I know."

"Then why—"

"Because someone has to stay. Someone has to make sure this looks right. And if they're going to find anyone, better it's me." She looked at David. "You have a life to go back to. A job. Family. Mike needs you. I don't—"

The truth lodged in her throat.

"I don't have anything to go back to."

Mike's face twisted. "Perse—"

"Go. Before someone drives by. Before someone sees."

David stood. Mike helped him. Guided him toward the door like leading a child.

At the threshold, Mike turned back. "Persephone. I'm sorry. About Iphy. I'm so sorry."

She nodded. Couldn't speak.

"I should have been watching her."

"We both should have been."

First time they'd said it. First time acknowledging the truth they'd spent years running from.

Mike's eyes filled. He didn't wipe the tears. "If I don't—if we don't make it—"

"You'll make it. Take care of him."

He nodded. Helped David down the porch stairs. Into the car.

Persephone watched from the doorway. Engine started. Taillights receded. Gone.


SCENE 8: FLIGHT INTO WOODS

She stood there for a long time. Or maybe no time at all. The sun had moved. Or hadn't. Hard to tell.

Finally she turned back to the house. James's body hadn't moved. Of course it hadn't. Dead things don't move. That's what makes them dead.

She should feel something. Grief. Horror. Guilt. Something.

She felt nothing.

The implant whispered: *DISSOCIATIVE STATE DETECTED. RECOMMENDED ACTION: SEEK MEDICAL ATTENTION. PROBABILITY OF COMPLIANCE: 0.002%.*

She grabbed her jacket from the chair. Her boots by the door. Water bottle from the kitchen. Didn't look at James. Couldn't.

Left the door open behind her. No locking up. No closing. No pretending she'd ever come back.

The woods were waiting.

She walked toward the tree line. Heard it before she saw it: birdsong. Thrush. Chickadee. Something else. Something golden.

Her implant flickered: *VERMIVORA CHRYSOPTERA DETECTED. GOLDEN-WINGED WARBLER. STATUS: FUNCTIONALLY EXTINCT. CURRENT POPULATION: <400 BREEDING PAIRS. PROBABILITY OF SURVIVAL: 0.003%.*

*ANOMALY: CALL PATTERN INCONSISTENT WITH BREEDING BEHAVIOR. POSSIBLE NETWORK ACTIVITY DETECTED. THEY KNOW. THEY KNOW. THEY KNOW.*

She walked faster.

The trees closed around her like hands. Like water. Like the mouth of something vast and patient and inevitable.

Behind her, the farmhouse grew smaller. James's body cooling on the floor. The door open to weather and flies and time.

Ahead, the Golden-winged Warbler called again.

*Come find me. Come find me. Come die.*

She walked on.
