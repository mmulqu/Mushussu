CHAPTER 12: AGAMEMNON'S RETURN

SCENE 1: MIKE'S ARRIVAL

She hasn't left the couch since the market. Two days, maybe three. Time moves like sludge. James brings soup she doesn't eat. The ceiling is mapped with water stains—continents she will never visit.

The car sound reaches her before consciousness does. Gravel. Engine. Wrong rhythm. Not James's truck.

Her implant flickers.

*Incoming data. Facial recognition scanning. Processing. Match probability 0.97. Identity: Michael Castellanos. Status: Known associate. Threat level: Calculating...*

She blinks. The overlay hangs in her vision, translucent, insistent. Mike. Here.

The door doesn't knock. It opens.

James is in the kitchen—she hears the spoon drop. Sees him turn, freeze. A man stands in the doorway. Gaunt. Unshaven. Hollow-eyed. Clothes dirt-creased, unwashed. Mike, but not Mike. A sketch of Mike. An underexposed photograph.

He doesn't look at James. Walks directly to her.

"Perse. I need you to come with me."

His voice: flat, airless. She tries to sit up. Her body won't respond.

"Mike." The word comes out wrong. "How did you—?"

"You told me. Before. Remember?" He kneels beside the couch. Too close. "You said if everything fell apart, you'd go back to the coast. Count the birds."

She never said this. The implant whispers: *False memory. Confabulation detected. Recommendation: Proceed with caution.*

James moves from the kitchen, stands between them. "I think you should leave."

Mike finally looks at him. Blank. No recognition. "Who the fuck are you?"

"I live here."

"This is Persephone's place now. I'm taking her home."

Persephone's throat tightens. "Mike—the rocket—weren't you supposed to be—?"

"They took me off." His eyes don't focus. "Last minute. Replaced me. Parker got my seat. It exploded three days after launch. They're all dead."

The room tilts. She grips the couch cushion. Ares I. The mission. Gone.

"I'm sorry," she whispers.

"Don't be sorry. Come with me."

James steps closer. "She's not going anywhere. You need to leave."

Mike stands. Taller than James. Thinner. Breakable. "I'm not leaving without her."

SCENE 2: HUSBAND'S ARRIVAL

The standoff holds. Mike on his feet, swaying slightly. James watchful, tense. Persephone pressed into the couch, paralyzed.

Ten minutes. Maybe fifteen. No one speaks.

Then: another car. They all hear it.

The door opens without knocking. A second man enters.

Clean-shaven. Exhausted. Dress shirt wrinkled, sleeves rolled. He sees Mike and his whole body sags.

"Oh, thank god. Mike. Mike, look at me."

Mike doesn't turn.

The man crosses the room—ignores James, ignores Persephone—kneels in front of Mike. Hands on Mike's face, forcing eye contact.

"Baby, it's me. It's David. You left. Three days ago. You turned off your phone. I've been—" His voice cracks. "I've been so scared."

Mike blinks. Slow. "I had to find her."

David looks at Persephone. She sees it in his face: recognition. Resentment. Grief.

"You're Persephone. From the lab."

She nods.

He turns back to Mike. "We need to go home. You need help. Real help. Come with me."

"I'm not leaving without Persephone."

"Persephone doesn't need to come. Just you and me. Like always."

"She's the only one who understands. About Iphy."

The name lands like a stone. David flinches. Persephone closes her eyes.

James, still standing, clears his throat. "I don't know who you are, but—"

David stands. "I'm his husband. And you are?"

"I already told him. I live here. Persephone's staying with me."

David's expression hardens. "How long has she been here?"

"A few weeks."

"And you just—what? Took her in?"

"She needed a place. Away from—" James gestures vaguely. "All of it."

David looks between them. Calculating. "Away from what, exactly?"

SCENE 3: JAMES INTERVENES

James crosses his arms. "From people like you. Showing up unannounced. Making demands."

"I'm not making demands." David's voice stays level. Controlled. "I'm trying to take my husband home before he hurts himself."

"Then take him and go."

"I'm trying to."

"Not hard enough."

David's jaw tightens. "Excuse me?"

"You heard me. He's been here twenty minutes. You've been here five. If you actually wanted to help him, you'd have gotten him out already."

"Don't tell me how to help my husband."

"I'm not telling you anything. I'm asking you to leave. Both of you."

Persephone finds her voice. "James, stop."

He doesn't look at her. "No. You don't owe them anything. Not after—"

He stops. Too late.

David's eyes narrow. "After what?"

James hesitates. "After what happened."

"What happened?"

"To your daughter."

The air leaves the room.

David goes very still. "How do you know about that?"

"Persephone told me."

David turns to her. "You told him? You never told anyone. Not even the therapist after—"

"I told him," Persephone says. Quiet. True.

David's face changes. Suspicion replacing shock. "What else did you tell him?"

SCENE 4: THE ACCUSATION

"That's between us," James says.

David steps toward him. "Is it? Because my husband just drove six hundred miles in the middle of a psychotic break to find her. And she's here. In your house. And you know things about our family that no one should know."

"She needed someone to talk to."

"Why you?"

"Because I listened."

"Bullshit." David's voice rises. "Who are you? Really?"

James doesn't answer.

David's breathing quickens. "She came here running from something. What was she running from?"

"From all of you. The lab. NASA. The fucking surveillance state."

"You don't know anything about her."

"I know she can't be around AI anymore. I know the implant was killing her. I know she needed to disappear."

David laughs—bitter, sharp. "And you just happened to be here? In the middle of nowhere? Waiting for some traumatized woman to show up and tell you her life story?"

"It wasn't like that."

"Then what was it like?"

Silence.

David's eyes widen. "Oh my god. You're one of them."

James goes still.

"The network people. Mike told me before he left. He said Persephone was talking about some—some animal consciousness group. In the woods. Drugging people. Making them think they could talk to birds."

"That's not—"

"Did you drug her?"

"It was consensual—"

"Did. You. Drug. Her."

Persephone tries to stand. Fails. "He didn't force me—"

"Jesus Christ." David's hands shake. "How long? How long has she been here?"

"Three weeks," James says.

"And how long have you been feeding her—what? Psilocybin? Ayahuasca?"

"She asked for it."

"She's not capable of asking for anything! Look at her!" David gestures at Persephone on the couch. "Does she look okay to you?"

James doesn't look.

David's voice drops. Dangerous. "You broke him."

"What?"

"Mike. You broke him. He was fine until she disappeared. Then he started losing it. Paranoid. Obsessive. Convinced they were coming for him too. He lost the mission because of it. They took him off Ares because he couldn't pass the psych eval. His career is over. His life is over. And now he's—" He gestures at Mike, still standing motionless. "He's gone. And it's because of you."

"I didn't break anyone."

"You took her away and you BROKE HIM—"

SCENE 5: THE MURDER

David lunges. Fast. Grabs James by the shirt, slams him against the wall. Picture frame falls. Glass shatters.

Persephone screams: "Stop—"

James pushes back. "Get off me—"

David punches him. Once. Hard. James's nose breaks—blood bright, sudden. He stumbles sideways, catches himself on the kitchen counter. His hand reaches for the knife block.

David sees it. Grabs James's wrist. They struggle—brief, ugly. The knife clatters to the floor.

David gets his hands around James's throat.

James claws at David's hands. Gasping. Eyes wide. Feet scrabbling for purchase.

"David—DAVID STOP—" Persephone is on her feet now, stumbling forward, too weak, too slow.

"He destroyed everything—" David's voice is ragged. Tears on his face. "Everything we built—"

James's face goes purple. Veins bulging. Mouth open. No sound.

Persephone pulls at David's arm. He doesn't move. She might as well be pulling at a tree.

Mike watches from across the room. Doesn't intervene. Doesn't speak. Just watches.

James stops moving.

David holds on. Thirty seconds. A minute. Making sure.

Then he lets go.

James collapses. Slides down the wall. Lands in a heap. Eyes open. Staring. Bruises darkening on his throat like continents.

Silence.

David backs away. Looks at his hands. Shaking. "Oh god. Oh god, what did I—"

He falls to his knees. Sobs.

Persephone stands frozen. Staring at James's body. The blood from his nose. The stillness. The impossible stillness.

Her implant flickers: *Target vital signs: Absent. Classification: Deceased. Recommendation: Contact emergency services.*

She doesn't move.

SCENE 6: AFTERMATH

Time fractures. David on the floor, hands over his face, shaking. Mike standing by the wall, blank. Persephone still staring.

Then Mike speaks. "We should go now."

David looks up. "What?"

"Get your things. We're leaving."

"I—" David stares at James's body. "I killed him. I have to call—we have to—"

Mike walks to David. Kneels. Puts a hand on his shoulder. "You were protecting me. That's what you always do."

"I killed him."

"He was going to kill you. You saw the knife."

Persephone's voice, small: "That's not what happened."

Mike looks at her. "That's what happened. David was defending himself."

"Mike, stop—" David's voice breaks.

"Get up. We're going."

"I can't—"

"You can. You will. Before someone sees."

David doesn't move.

Mike stands. Turns to Persephone. "Get your things."

"We have to call the police," she says.

"No."

"James is dead. We have to—"

"He was going to kill David. You saw it. The knife."

"That's not—" But she stops. Because she did see the knife. James reaching for it. Did he reach first? Or was it after David grabbed him?

Her implant flickers: *Memory reconstruction in progress. Accuracy: 62%. Conflicting data. Unable to verify sequence.*

Mike's voice, steady: "Persephone. Do you want David to go to prison?"

She looks at David. Sobbing on the floor. Looks at Mike. Hollow and certain.

"No," she whispers.

"Then get your things. We're leaving."

SCENE 7: THE CHOICE

Her implant cycles through calculations.

*Moral framework analysis: Utilitarianism—greatest good for greatest number. Outcome A: Contact authorities. David incarcerated. Mike institutionalized. James remains deceased. Net benefit: 0. Outcome B: Flee. David avoids incarceration. Mike receives informal care. James remains deceased. Net benefit: +2. Recommendation: Outcome B.*

*Alternative framework: Deontological ethics. Duty to report crime. Duty to protect. Duties in conflict. ERROR. Unable to resolve.*

She looks at James's body. Eyes open. Bruises blooming. He drugged her. Groomed her. Used her for his cult's purposes. Does he deserve justice? Does she owe him that?

She looks at David. Destroyed. A man who killed to protect the person he loves. Terrible love. But love.

She looks at Mike. Broken because the systems broke him. NASA. The mission. The same machines that killed Iphy.

Iphy. Underwater. Eyes open.

Persephone closes her eyes.

"Okay."

Mike: "Okay what?"

"Okay, we go. But not together. You two take David's car. Drive south. Find a hospital in Massachusetts. Tell them David had a breakdown. Check him in."

"What about you?"

"I'll stay. Clean up. Make it look like James left. Then I'll go into the woods."

David looks up. "We can't just leave you—"

"You have to. If we're all together, they'll find us. If I'm separate, I can—I can lead them away."

Mike studies her. "The cult. They'll find you."

"I know."

"And you're okay with that?"

She opens her eyes. "I don't know. But it's better than you two going to prison."

Mike nods. "Okay."

"Okay?"

"You're right. It's the best option."

David stares at both of them. "This is insane. We can't—"

"We can." Mike's voice is gentle. Final. "Get up, David. We're going."

SCENE 8: FLIGHT INTO WOODS

Mike lifts David to his feet. Guides him to the door. David stumbles, unsteady, still staring back at James's body.

Persephone watches from the doorway.

At the car, Mike pauses. Looks back at her.

"Perse. I'm sorry. About Iphy. I'm so sorry."

She nods. Can't speak.

"I should have been watching her."

"We both should have been," she says.

The first time they've acknowledged it. Shared guilt. Shared failure.

Mike's eyes fill. "If I don't—if we don't make it—"

"You'll make it. Take care of him."

He nods. Gets David into the passenger seat. Starts the engine.

The car pulls away. Gravel crunching. Taillights fading.

Persephone stands on the porch. Watches until they're gone.

Then she turns back to the house. The open door. James's body inside. She should clean up. She should call someone. She should—

She doesn't.

Instead, she walks to the bedroom. Grabs her jacket. Boots. Water bottle. Binoculars.

Leaves the door open behind her. No locking up. No return.

Walks toward the trees.

Birdsong rises. Chickadee. Thrush. And beneath them, distant: the bright, liquid trill of a Golden-winged Warbler.

Her implant flickers: *Incoming data. Network activity detected. Proximity alert. They know they know they know they—*

She walks faster.

The trees close around her. November light filtering through bare branches. The forest floor soft with decay. She doesn't look back.

The warbler calls again. Closer now.

She follows it into the dark.
