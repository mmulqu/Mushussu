CHAPTER 12: AGAMEMNON'S RETURN

The sound came first—gravel under tires, the engine's low idle.

Persephone was on the couch where she'd been for two days. James had brought her soup. Crackers. Water she couldn't drink. The market still clung to her—the smell of meat, the cellular horror, the crowd's faces melting into animal masks. Her implant flickered uselessly, trying to modulate what couldn't be modulated.

James was in the kitchen. She heard him chopping something. Carrots, maybe. The domestic rhythm felt obscene after what she'd seen.

The car door slammed.

James stopped chopping.

Persephone's implant sparked: *incoming data—facial recognition—Mike Castellanos—probability 0.97*

She sat up. The room tilted.

The front door opened without a knock.

Mike stood in the doorway. Gaunt. Unshaven. Eyes like burnt-out windows. His clothes were wrinkled, stained with something dark—coffee or dirt or both. He looked at her and through her at the same time.

"Perse," he said. "I need you to come with me."

James appeared in the kitchen doorway, knife still in hand. He set it down carefully on the counter. "Can I help you?"

Mike didn't look at him. He walked directly toward Persephone, crossing the room like a man underwater—slow, dream-logic movements.

"Mike," Persephone said. Her voice was hoarse. "How did you find me?"

"You told me." Mike's voice was flat. "Before. You said if everything fell apart, you'd go back to the coast. Count the birds."

She'd never said this.

"Mike, weren't you—the rocket—"

"They took me off." He blinked slowly. "Last minute. Replaced me. It exploded. They're all dead."

James stepped between them. "I think you should leave."

Mike finally looked at him. Really looked. "Who the fuck are you?"

"I'm the person who lives here."

"This is Persephone's place now." Mike's jaw tightened. "I'm taking her home."

"Mike—" Persephone tried to stand, but her legs wouldn't hold. "You can't—"

"Home?" James's voice was careful, controlled. "Persephone's staying here. She's safe here."

"Safe?" Mike laughed—a strange, hollow sound. "Safe from what?"

"From NASA. From the surveillance state. From all the people who want to control her."

Mike went still. His eyes focused suddenly, sharply. "What did you say?"

"I said she's safe here. From people like you."

"People like me?"

"NASA. The lab. The implant people. The ones who put that thing in her head."

Mike's hands curled into fists. "How do you know about the implant?"

James said nothing.

"How do you KNOW?" Mike's voice cracked. He started pacing—three steps, turn, three steps. "Who are you? Really?"

"I already told you—"

"No. No no no." Mike stopped. "You just happened to be here? In the middle of nowhere? Waiting for her?"

"I wasn't waiting—"

"What are you? Some kind of cult?"

James went rigid.

Mike stared at him. "Oh my god. You're one of them. The network people."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"Yes you do. Persephone told me. Before she disappeared. Some animal consciousness group. Drugging people. Making them think they could talk to birds."

Persephone found her voice. "Mike, it's not—"

"Did you drug her?" Mike took a step toward James.

"It was consensual—"

"Did. You. Drug. Her."

Silence filled the room like water.

"He didn't force me," Persephone whispered.

"Jesus Christ." Mike's voice broke. "Jesus fucking Christ. You broke her. She was fine until she met you. Then she left the lab. Then she disappeared. Then I started—" His voice caught. "I lost the mission because of you. They took me off the crew because I was obsessed with finding her. My career is over. My life is over. And Persephone—what did you DO to her?"

"I loved her—"

"You DRUGGED her—"

Mike lunged.

His hands grabbed James's shirt, slammed him against the wall. A framed photo fell, glass shattering.

"Mike—MIKE STOP—" Persephone was screaming but the sound seemed far away.

James tried to push him off. "Get off me—"

Mike punched him. James's nose broke with a wet crack. Blood sprayed down his chin.

James stumbled toward the kitchen, reaching for the counter, for the knife block. His hand closed around a handle.

Mike saw it. Grabbed James's wrist. They struggled—two men grappling in slow motion, the knife between them.

The knife fell.

Mike's hands found James's throat.

"Mike—MIKE PLEASE—" Persephone tried to pull at his arms but she was too weak, still recovering, her hands useless.

"He destroyed everything—" Mike was sobbing and strangling James at the same time. "He took you away—Iphy—the rocket—everything—"

James's face went purple. His hands clawed at Mike's fingers. His eyes bulged.

Persephone pulled harder but Mike was immovable, a machine, a thing without reason.

James stopped moving.

Mike held on. Thirty more seconds. Making sure.

Then he let go.

James collapsed onto the floor like discarded clothing.

Silence.

Mike leaned back against the wall. His chest heaved. His hands shook. He looked at them—knuckles bleeding, rope-burned from James's nails.

"Oh god," he whispered. "Oh god. What did I do?"

Persephone stood frozen. James's body was sprawled at an impossible angle. His eyes were open. Bruises bloomed dark on his throat.

"I didn't—" Mike stared at his hands. "I didn't mean to—he was going to—the knife—he was—"

"Mike."

He looked up at her. His face was wet.

"He took you. He drugged you. He—"

"Mike." Her voice was steady. "James is dead."

"We have to call the police." Mike fumbled for his phone. "I killed him. I have to—I have to call them."

"Yes," Persephone said. "We have to call them."

"They'll arrest me." His voice was small, childlike. "Prison. I can't—Perse, I can't—"

"You killed him, Mike."

"It was self-defense. You saw it. The knife."

"That's not what happened."

"He was reaching for the knife—"

"After you attacked him."

Silence again. Mike stared at James's body. At the bruises. At his own hands.

"I'm sorry," he whispered. "I'm so sorry. About everything. About Iphy. About—"

"I know."

Persephone looked at James—his kind face, now slack. His blood on the floor. Then at Mike—destroyed, sobbing, broken by forces neither of them could name.

Her implant flickered: *moral calculus loading—utilitarian ism—deontology—virtue ethics—ERROR ERROR ERROR*

If she called the police: Mike arrested. Prison or institution. James still dead. Nothing fixed.

If she didn't call: Murder unsolved. She's complicit. But Mike might survive.

What would Iphy want? (Underwater eyes, hair floating like seaweed.)

James drugged her. Groomed her. Used her for his cult. Does he deserve justice? Does she owe him that?

Mike killed to protect her. Terrible love. But love.

Mike is broken because the systems broke him. NASA. Capitalism. The same machines that drowned Iphy.

She can't save James. She can maybe save Mike.

"You have to leave," Persephone said. "Now."

Mike stared at her. "I can't—I have to call—"

"Mike. Get in your car. Drive south. Find a hospital in Massachusetts. Check yourself in. Tell them you're having a breakdown."

"What about you?"

"I'll stay. Clean up. Make it look like James left. Then I'll disappear."

"They'll find you. The cult."

"I know."

"Perse—"

"GO. Before someone sees."

Mike stood. His legs shook. He looked at James one more time, then at Persephone.

"I love you," he said.

She said nothing.

He walked out. The door hung open behind him.

Persephone listened to his car start, back up, drive away. The gravel sound fading into silence.

She looked at James's body. Felt nothing. Dissociated. Watching herself from very far away.

She grabbed her jacket. Her boots. A water bottle.

Left the door open. Symbolic—no locking up, no return.

Walked toward the woods.

Birdsong rose around her—thrush, warbler, chickadee. Spring migration in full chorus.

Her implant: *incoming data—network activity—they know they know they know*

She walked faster.

The trees closed around her like water. Hemlock and pine and the darkness between them.

In the distance: a Golden-winged Warbler call. High, buzzing. Unmistakable.

She'd found them. Or they'd found her.

Persephone walked deeper into the trees. The farmhouse disappeared behind her. James's body on the floor. Mike fleeing south. The whole ruined world falling away.

Only the woods remained. And the birds. And the thing waiting for her in the darkness between the trunks.
