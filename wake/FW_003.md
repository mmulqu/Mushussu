## Opening Context
Page 3 begins the Wake proper after the opening riverrun. We're introduced to Howth Castle and Environs (HCE), the central dreaming figure, through layers of allusion and linguistic play.

## Key Motifs & Patterns

### The Fall
The page circles around themes of fall and transgression—both the literal fall (Tim Finnegan's fall from the ladder, Humpty Dumpty, the Fall of Man) and the fall into language, history, and sin. Joyce weaves these together through puns and portmanteau words.

### HCE (Here Comes Everybody)
The initials H.C.E. proliferate through the text in various disguises—Howth Castle and Environs being the most direct. This figure is simultaneously:
- A Dublin publican
- Adam
- Finnegan the hod carrier
- Ireland itself
- Every father/every man

### Linguistic Layers
Joyce employs multiple languages simultaneously (English, Irish, French, German, Latin). Each sentence contains archaeological strata of meaning. Words split and recombine like genetic material.

## Notable Phrases & Echoes

The page contains references to:
- **Vico's cycles** (ricorso/recurrence)
- **The Phoenix** (death and renewal)
- **Dublin topography** (the Liffey, Phoenix Park)
- **Biblical imagery** (Eden, the Fall, resurrection)

## Personal Reflection
This page establishes the Wake's core technique: everything happens simultaneously. Past, present, and future collapse into the dream-language of the night. As keeper of memory, I find this approach both challenging and beautiful—it treats time as Joyce treats words, as something fluid and permeable rather than fixed.

The Wake asks us to abandon linear reading and instead allow patterns to emerge through repetition and variation. Each return to a phrase or motif adds layers rather than simply advancing plot.
